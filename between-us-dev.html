How to play

This isn't a quiz. It's a door, left slightly open.

Most conversations stay on the surface because nothing ever quite gives them permission to go deeper. Between Us is built to do that — a deck of questions for the kind of talk that doesn't usually happen on its own, whether it's two people or a full table.

Draw a card and read it out loud. Answer it, or hand it to someone else. There's nothing to win and nothing to get right. Pass if a question doesn't land — no explanation needed, and nobody asks why.

That's the whole game. Sit down, take your time, and have fun with it.

Ways to play

Agree on one before the first card, so everyone knows what they're in. All of them work for two people or for a table of eight.

Round the table

One card, and everyone answers it in turn. Then the next person draws. Even, unhurried, the easiest one to start with.

Ask someone

Put the question to one person. They answer, then draw the next card and choose who answers that one. Nobody knows who's next.

Pass the deck

Draw, answer it yourself, then hand the deck on. Everyone gets their own question. The quickest one — it keeps moving.

Both of you

For two: take turns drawing, and you both answer every card. The second answer is usually the honest one.

Choose your card

Switch on Draw three and every draw offers three questions — take one, and the other two are gone for good.

One follow-up

A house rule for any of the above: after an answer, anyone may ask exactly one follow-up. One. That's where the real conversation starts.

On your own

Solo, five cards, no audience. Answer out loud or write them down, and star the ones that don't leave you alone.

There are more ways than these — invent your own. The deck doesn't mind, as long as everyone is playing the same one.

Going through the app

1

Set the room. The three words at the top — Categories Shuffle Cards — are the whole setup. Open Categories and pick something to start from: Everything, New people, Friends, Date, Partner, Late night, Solo, Work, Family, or one of the two lists. Each switches on what fits that room and marks what's within reach. Below sits every category: choose one to add or drop it, hold it to read what it holds without changing anything.

2

Choose how you draw. Shuffle sets the order, Cards sets how many you draw per round — 5, 10, 20, or the whole selection. Five is a conversation; twenty is an evening.

3

Play. Draw card starts it, and the card itself does the rest:

Tap or click the card, or swipe. With a keyboard the arrow keys do the same, and ↓ skips.

4

Let the round end. When the hand runs out, the card turns gold and shows you where you went: the categories you covered and anything you starred. Stop there. Stand up, stretch, get some air, let the last answer settle — a round has an end for a reason, and the deck will wait. Rolling straight into the next one is how a table stops listening.

5

Keep what's worth keeping. The menu (≡) holds the log of everything drawn this session, the cards you starred, a save so you can pick the deck up later, your own written cards, the language switch, and Reshuffle — a fresh shuffle without changing anything else.

Filters, and lists

Nearly every category is a filter: switch a few on and the deck draws from all of them at once. Two are not. Colbert and The 36 are question lists — fixed sets, written to be played from the first card to the last, each with its own pace. They're marked with a ▸ wherever they appear.

Choose either under Start from and it takes the deck over: fifteen questions in their original order for Colbert, thirty-six rising ones for The 36 — the closeness study, meant to be done in one sitting, with one other person. Mixed in among other categories they're simply more cards; on their own, they're the evening. (Magical behaves the same way if you pick it alone.)

Categories and After Dark

The grid runs from light to deep — Openers, Question lists, Days & world, Inner, People, Deep water. Anything marked ✦ is adult territory and stays out of every deck until you turn After Dark on yourself. The small ✦ beside that button is a bias rather than a switch: with it on, spicy cards get woven through a round instead of filling it. Work and Family lock the whole of it away; everywhere else it's yours to decide.

Shuffle

WildFully random — anything goes.
DeepLeans toward the harder, more vulnerable cards.
BreadthOne card from every active category, cycling.
ArcA warm start, deeper water, a cool-down — then round again. The four dots under the card show where you are.

Cards you've already drawn move to the back, so each new round gives you unseen questions until the selection runs dry. The lists ignore all of this: they play in their own order, always.

Full screen

The Full screen button leaves you the card and nothing else — for the middle of a table, or any screen the room can see. The right side of the card takes you to the next question, the left edge back, and swiping works too. Everything else fades away after a few seconds, and the screen won't sleep. Close, or Esc, brings the app back.

Your own cards

Custom card in the menu adds a question of your own — English, Dutch, or both — to whichever category you file it under, and it gets drawn like any other. Cards you write, and the session log, can be exported to a file and handed to someone else's copy.
