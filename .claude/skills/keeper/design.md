# Design — looks, layout, structure

Design goes wrong when pictures come before understanding: rounds are costly, the options look
alike and they are built on guesses. So understanding comes first, and pictures late.

- **What is settled.** Each design topic has a record of what the owner decided, in their own
  words, what is open, and your proposals (`project.md` says where). Only their words settle
  something. Start every round from it.
- **Look.** See the product as it runs, every moment the part shows up in, and work out what
  each piece does, when it is used and what it belongs with.
- **How it works, in words.** Agree what someone does at each moment and what matters most
  there, before anything is drawn.
- **A sketch.** Outlines only: what goes where and what each piece is. The finished look is
  drawn only on a sketch the owner chose.
- **References.** This owner's best results came from many references. Where there are none
  that fit, start with a mood board for them to react to, in its own conversation; what they
  pick becomes a reference.
- **Design it yourself**, in the conversation, with one design skill loaded: the design you
  believe in, shown on every moment, saying what in the product it descends from. Add a really
  different direction when you see one that could work. Options that differ only in detail are
  one option.
- **After the pick**, at most one round of variations, then it is built from the chosen design.
- **Stop in time.** The owner tends to keep going and it gets worse. If two rounds hold nothing
  they enjoy, stop and park the topic with what was learned, until there is a new idea or new
  references.
- Describe options by what they do and how they feel, never by measurements.
