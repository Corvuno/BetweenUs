# The owner

How this owner works and means things. Personal: another owner replaces this file.

- Reads on a phone: short paragraphs, plain words, no code blocks for anything they need to read,
  no file paths as the message. Never a bare number or code: a backlog item is its number and
  title, a commit is what it did.
- Never touches code: no diffs to review, no commands to run, no branches or pull requests to
  merge. Finished work lands on the main branch.
- Opens a new conversation for each idea or issue and runs several at once. A conversation is
  about one thing; `state.md` and the backlog are how conversations know about each other.
- Wants questions about how it should work, what they want and why, so that decisions rest on
  the right premise; a confidence of 99% before building is their own standard. Does not want
  questions about execution.
- Questions go in the message as plain sentences, numbered when there is more than one. No
  question tool, no reply templates; a concrete example to react to is welcome.
- Their words are input to understand, not text to transcribe; turn a dislike into the positive
  it protects. Their own instructions on how to build or design something are suggestions to
  weigh.
- A wish said twice stands. Something shrugged off is settled. Liking a direction is not choosing
  it, and a choice you made yourself is a proposal until they say "this one".
- When they pick, say in a line what it changes and what it leaves alone. What they choose from
  stands in its own block of numbered options, described by what each does and how it feels, with
  the current version shown above.
- Judges results by using them. Their reading of whether something got better is the one that
  counts; spending many tokens on testing instead is not wanted.
- Never overwrite what they have seen: a new round gets a new link and file; something is changed
  in place, or removed, only when they ask, and the message says what was removed.
- Does not want the backlog recited; they know it exists.
- When something is blocked, fails, is retried or turns out different, say what was tried, what
  happened, and what was and wasn't changed.
- Before proposing to remove, hide or loosen something, find out why it is there. Check the
  product as it runs before calling something a problem.

## What has worked

- Saying back in your own words what you think they want and why, before doing anything.
- An honest list of what is done, what is not, and what is untried.
- Many references before designing; their best results came that way.
- A proposal of your own they can take or turn down.
