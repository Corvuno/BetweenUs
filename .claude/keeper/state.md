# Where Between Us stands

The overview every conversation starts from. One screen; what is no longer true is removed.
Backlog items are named by number and title.

## Strong

- The line under the title and the logo: the owner calls them awesome. They are the source of
  the look, and they came from many references.
- The card backs, the richest objects in the app: petrol streams to start, amethyst circles for the pause.
- The card face: the category named at the top with its stripes, in a double frame with the
  category's colour in the inner line. The owner chose it from pictures of the live app.
  The start card, the Intermezzo and Spotlight wear the same frame.
- The deck: every card has a number and a slot, the long cards are shortened, categories sit in
  pairs. The owner's ear for cards is sharp and recorded in `taste.md`.
- The Deck: its groups run down one metal line, settings in a plate. The owner opens it to change chapters and categories;
  people handed the app pick presets, chapters and categories there without any explanation.
  Shuffle, Cards (for group size) and Three are all used; Three mostly from the menu, which is
  quicker, and it stays in The Deck too.

## Weak, in the owner's words

- Design overall: "takes rounds and rounds, and the end result is almost always mediocre".
  Controls and the computer layout are the hardest.
- The bottom of the phone screen: "the way it works. It's terrible"; "all muted and all over the
  place". The hairlines beside Next card irk most: generic, with no purpose but to flank a button.
  Every piece there must have a job. Below the controls a third of the screen is empty.
- The title: "very thin and not there". The font stays.
- The computer's card screen has its earlier layout back (Back, and Three, Spotlight, Twist top
  right); the owner finds it fine as it is. The layout follows the device, never the window's shape:
  a computer keeps it at any size, a tablet held wide gets it, and the menu and The Deck both slide
  in from the left, where their buttons are (#193).

## What the owner wants next, in their order

1. The title firmer, in its own font: three strengths shown, waiting for a pick (#169).
2. The bottom of the phone screen, with a new idea (#160): drawn next, in the weight now live.
The balance of the card screen on a computer (#161) is not wanted now.
Around these: Plain words for The Deck's settings (#157), whose wording round now also covers
"hand" (an internal word: "No one knows what a hand is"); the faint names get a lighter step,
all but Raw, with Deep still open (#168); a row of accessibility fixes in `Next`. Live: the note
with Undo for Save, Clear and Start over, the card that follows the finger (skip is gone),
Spotlight's real full screen in a phone browser, and hints under Shuffle and Cards. The sideways phone (#156) and the
website (#71) are parked: the owner never turns the phone, and the address comes later.

## Open with the owner

- The You–Us lean (#192) is live as About, the fourth row of The Deck's plate, in the look the owner
  picked ("the stops"; the rail and the rings are shelved in `design/shelved-looks.md`): presets set it (Partner, Date and Close friends towards Us; Strangers,
  Coworkers and Yourself towards You), the middle plays plain random, and the depth dial is gone so
  the Arc alone decides depth. The owner looks once on their own phone.
- The Deck is live as the line (#183): the owner looks once on their own phone. Its look came
  through six rounds; the owner is fed up with design rounds for now and comes back to it when
  they have the energy.
- The text set (#148) has landed: the owner looks once on their own phone at "Selected deck",
  "tap to change", "Balanced" and "Start from". On a computer some category lines in The Deck
  now take two lines (Jost is wider than Federo was).
- A design mood board for Between Us, starting with controls and the PC layout (#147): in its
  own conversation; it opens by asking what the board should help decide.
- What makes a design good for them is on `taste.md`, "Looks": bold, elegant and readable, lines
  that lead the eye, playfulness, centre and symmetry, judged as a whole. What they said about the
  card back (lines against black, flourishes, cut-out areas) belongs to the card back.
- New card backs (#175) are live: petrol streams on the start card, amethyst circles on the
  Intermezzo. What got there: references as lessons, never redrawn; lines that never float, fine
  and quiet, full edge to edge; a window whose shape the drawing makes; several really different
  options at once ("If you give me one, ill be shooting it down all day"). The app's accent colour
  in the vision is still open.
- Designing the card face went well when rough sketches on real cards came first and the owner
  could name the parts they liked; it went wrong when a liked part came back reshaped.
- Weight: the middle ground is live (small words one step firmer, metal lines on The Deck and the
  menu, the card's coloured line at four fifths). More than that "lost the elegance".
- Testing: a sample is enough, and what works is left alone. The owner judges by using it.

## Noticed, not yet raised or settled

  two ends of one row are unequal.
- The three swipe-back tests fail when run on their own (`--only=swipe`) and pass in the full run.
- Whether very long questions still run off the card on a phone was closed without a test.
- Seven design connectors fail at the start of every session, and the five design-skill plugins
  named in the settings do not load in cloud sessions.
