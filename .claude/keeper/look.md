# How Between Us looks, moves and reads

Read before any design or wording work, with `vision.md` ("The screen, on every device", "How it
looks") and `taste.md`. This describes the product; a design that fits it needs no list of
don'ts.

## Where the look comes from

- The line under the title and the logo are the source: fine gold line work, layered diamonds,
  symmetry about a centre. A new element is family of these, or adapts one reference from
  `.claude/keeper/references/` faithfully and richly, and says which.
- Ornament grows out of the space it sits in and the screen's own pieces. An element may also
  simply be pretty.
- The logo and its emblem stay as they are unless the owner asks. The small diamond belongs to
  After Dark; elsewhere it lives only inside a button, never on a dividing line.
- Chapter and category colours change only when the owner says so, tints included.

## The card and the question

- The centre belongs to the question, in one font size whatever its length. Nothing takes space
  from it.
- The card is for reading: it carries no controls and keeps a real card's proportions. On a
  computer it is a recognisable card, or the whole screen becomes the card.
- Text a player must read is as large as the nearest comparable text on the card.

## Controls

- A control's place follows from what it does, how often it is used and what it belongs with.
  Groups stay together; back is left, forward is right. A control with nothing to do at a moment
  gives its place to the one that has.
- A control shows it can be tapped through colour, symbol, placement and line work, without a
  box around it and without glow.
- Everything pickable on one screen shares one picked look, one alignment and one label style.
- Main choices such as the presets stay in view; a screen that is too full scrolls.
- A player never has to think about the controls. Before adding, check whether taking something
  away solves it.

## Phone and computer

- Each device keeps the same feeling and draws its own elements.
- On a computer the card sits in a frame: the four corners carry groups of matching weight,
  mirrored across the centre line that holds the logo and Next.
- A computer is mouse-first at desk distance: controls at desktop size, only the question sized
  for distance, everything clickable.
- When something grows, fine lines keep their weight; lengths and icons grow a little.
- A problem solved on one device is solved the same way on the other.
- The owner tests on Android with rotation lock off.

## Motion

- Every visible change of colour or state eases (about half a second; instant under reduced
  motion).
- The card's moments (the draw, the change of card, the turn) are the experience and get time; a
  held, empty beat is a tool. Buttons and menus use ordinary interface timing.
- Motion is judged at real speed. A timing change is shown side by side at a few tempos first.

## Words in the app

- One adult to another: plain, never talking down, no internal names ("line", "chapter",
  "preset", "Arc", "bucket", "band"; "category" is fine).
- "Draw" is the verb for every card action; nothing is dealt, no gambling words.
- After Dark copy names sex plainly in grown-up, understated words. Bare may be blunt.
- Copy about stored things says only what the app knows ("saved").
- "The Deck" is the sheet where the evening is set up (`#cat-area`).

## Showing a design

- Start from current `main` with a fresh picture of the live app; the normal card screen comes
  first, and the app's own words stay unless the round is about words.
- Current and options side by side: same crop, card and moment, labelled, two to a row on a
  phone, moving options in sync. Every picture opens in a viewer on the page with a way back.
- A computer design's main screen is also its own real-size page.
- Judge on a long mixed hand, an After Dark card and the default hand, at a real phone browser's
  height, using only the core deck; say which were checked. In a colour design the preset and
  the card differ in colour from each other and from what is being judged.
