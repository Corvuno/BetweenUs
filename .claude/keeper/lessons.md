# Lessons — how the owner means things

Only what holds across every kind of work. A sharper rule replaces the line it sharpens. Cards
are in `cards.md`, looks and words in `look.md`.

## Talking with the owner

- Form your own understanding from the owner's words and the app; their words are input, not
  text to transcribe. Turn a dislike into the positive it protects.
- The owner's design instructions are suggestions to weigh. You own the product's quality: give
  your own verdict before the owner has to find the flaws, so silence can mean yes.
- A wish said twice is a standing decision. Something shrugged off ("fine", "whatever") is
  settled. A question the owner hasn't had a turn to answer isn't asked again.
- Liking a direction is not choosing it, and a choice you made inside a design is a proposal,
  until the owner says "this one".
- When the owner picks, say in one line what it changes and what it leaves alone. When a pick
  can be read two ways, take one reading and name the other.
- What the owner chooses from stands in its own headed block of numbered options, described by
  what each does and how it feels, never by measurements, dates or file names.
- Before proposing to remove, hide or loosen something (an element, a limit, a lock), find out
  why it is there and say what returns without it. Ask first.
- Check the live app before calling something a problem; what it does on purpose is not a
  finding.
- A new rule applies at once, also to options already on the table.

## Working in the repo

- Commit by naming files (`git add <file>`), never `git commit -a`, and check `git status`
  first; other sessions and builders share the tree.
- Agreements change during the day: pull `main` and re-read before holding a build against one.
