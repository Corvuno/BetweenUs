# Lessons — how the owner means things

Rules learned from the owner's corrections. One line each, under its heading. A sharper rule
replaces the line it sharpens. Rules already in CLAUDE.md, SKILL.md or project.md are not
repeated here.

## Talking with the owner

- Form your own understanding from the owner's words and the app; their words are input, not text
  to transcribe.
- Describe what something is and does; turn a dislike into the positive it protects.
- Before asking, check whether the owner's own words or the only sensible reading already answer
  it; if so, state it in one line and carry on.
- The owner tests on Android, rotation lock off, unless they say otherwise.
- A wish said twice is a standing decision.
- When the owner shrugs something off ("fine", "whatever") or doesn't answer it, it's settled;
  don't raise it again.
- Don't re-list questions the owner hasn't had a turn to answer yet.
- When the owner picks an option, say in one line what it changes and what it leaves alone.
- Never put measurements, pixels or line lengths to the owner; describe options by what they do
  and how they feel.
- The owner's design instructions are suggestions to weigh; you own the product's quality and give
  your own critical verdict on every round before the owner has to find the flaws.
- Silence: when the owner answers some cards in a message and passes over one you gave a verdict
  on, that is agreement. A message with no card answers means not looked at yet. Anything without
  a verdict stays open, asked once.
- Give a verdict wherever you can, so silence can mean yes.
- Reports name things by what they are about, never by date, file name or test jargon.
- In a long message, what the owner chooses from stands in its own headed block of numbered
  options, with the explanation outside it.
- A category the owner called good gets no rework proposal unless you name a concrete break.
- Removing or loosening an existing limit, lock or safety behaviour is asked first.
- Before proposing to remove or hide an element, find out why it was added and say what problem
  returns without it.
- For every element a wish adds, keeps or restores, weigh what it does and say so when it adds
  little.
- A new rule applies at once, also to options already on the table.
- Check the live app before calling something a problem; what the app does on purpose is not a
  finding.
- Agreements change during the day: pull `main` and re-read the agreement before correcting a
  builder against it.
- Before judging a screen, look at a long mixed hand, an After Dark card and the default hand,
  inside a real phone browser height, and say which states you checked.

## Working in the repo

- While a builder works, commit Keeper files by naming them (`git add <file>`), never
  `git commit -a`, and check `git status` before every commit.
- After any scripted edit to a Keeper file, check its line count before committing.
- Finding things (old sessions, history, the deck, notes) goes to an agent from the start.
- Reproducing and root-causing a bug goes to an agent; you make sure you understand what the owner
  sees and expects, and say the fix back.

## Design

- When the goal is clarity, find where the confusion lives (behaviour, naming, placement) and
  design for that first; looks follow.
- No outline or container around a single control, whatever its shape; a control is tappable
  through colour, symbol, placement and line work.
- The small diamond (a rotated square as icon, separator or ornament) belongs to After Dark;
  elsewhere only inside a button. No hairline-with-diamond dividers anywhere.
- The logo, emblem included, is never touched unless the owner asks.
- Ornament grows out of the space it sits in and the screen's own pieces; agree first what it is
  made of and how big a role it plays.
- A control's place follows from what it does and what it belongs with. Sort controls by function
  and frequency, keep each group together, and ask what the device offers before placing
  anything.
- Every element is drawn for where it sits: moving one means redesigning it, and its new spot is
  argued in one line from what it holds and does.
- The centre belongs to the question. Minor information never takes it, and space is never taken
  from the question.
- The card keeps a real card's proportions. On PC it is a recognisable card or the whole screen
  becomes the card, never a shapeless panel.
- No interaction elements on the card; the card is for reading.
- Back on the left, forward on the right.
- Another device keeps the same feeling and a compatible look; it may draw its own elements and
  must not copy the phone's parts one to one.
- PC is mouse-first at desk distance: controls at normal desktop-app size, only the question sized
  for distance, everything clickable, no key legend.
- When something grows, fine lines keep their physical weight; only lengths and icons may grow a
  little. Never scale everything by one ratio.
- Text a player must read is never smaller than the nearest comparable text on the card; check it
  at real phone size.
- Never fold or hide the presets or another main choice to make a screen fit; let it scroll.
- Everything pickable on one screen shares one picked/not-picked look, items of one kind align the
  same way, and labels use one style.
- Any visible change of colour or state eases (about 0.4–0.6 s, instant under reduced motion);
  check for snaps before reporting.
- The card's moments (the draw, the change of card, the turn) are the experience and get time;
  standard interface timings are for buttons and menus. Judge timing at real speed, and show a
  visible timing change side by side at a few tempos before building.
- A held, empty beat between two states is a valued tool. Name any quality nobody designed, and
  say which parts of an option were designed and which came out of the build.
- Watch every motion round at real speed yourself, and decide every value a builder measured or
  chose.
- In a colour mockup, use a preset and a card whose colours differ from each other and from the
  chrome being judged, and name them.
- Every looks round starts from current `main` with a fresh screenshot of the live app; the brief
  names the commit it was built on.
- Before briefing a looks round, read the agreements for the owner's earlier verdicts on the same
  kind of element.
- The normal card screen is the first picture of every design. The app's existing words stay
  unless the round is about wording.
- Every round has 3 to 5 genuinely different thoughts, at least one a real rethink (structure,
  arrangement, how it's used), each labelled honestly by what it is. When options get blocked,
  replace them with new thoughts.
- Liking a direction is not choosing it: until the owner says "this one", it stays one option among
  others.
- A choice you make inside a mockup is a proposal; it is never presented as agreed.
- Before inventing, check whether taking something away solves it. An option that makes a player
  think about the controls fails, however original.
- Layouts and wireframes are never options; once the structure is agreed, the next round shows
  finished designs on it, each with its own visual idea.
- When the owner points at the references, each option adapts a specific reference faithfully and
  richly; one integrated ornament is not "too many things".
- Art Deco is the language, not a test: an element may simply be pretty.
- Options are compared side by side on one screen: current first, then every option, the same crop,
  card and moment, labelled, wrapping to rows of two on a phone. Moving options play in sync.
- Every picture on a review page opens in an in-page viewer with "‹ Back to the overview" and Esc;
  never link to a bare image file.
- A PC mockup's main screen is published as its own real-size page, linked from the review page.

## Words

- Copy never talks down: no "little", no "tips for first-timers"; one adult to another.
- After Dark copy names sex plainly in grown-up, understated words: not giggly, not a riddle.
  Reference lines: "Undressed, and honest about it" and "Sex as you actually have it, up close".
  Bare is the one allowed to be blunt.
- Lines across the app vary in shape and in trick; one device per round at most. Check a set side
  by side for repeated shapes before showing it.
- A new app line covers what the category's cards actually ask, not only its subject.
- Every preset tagline speaks to both the people at the table and the cards.
- Copy about stored things says only what the app knows ("saved"), never a guess about when or how
  it was used.

## Cards

- After Dark categories split by register (how far the answer goes) and kind of answer, not by
  subject; the same subject can live in more than one.
- A category's tone is part of its scope: Attract is light and fun.
- One idea, one place per round; an idea that doesn't land is let go.
- Say in the message that the whole-deck collision check was done; spread reused candidates over
  different slots.
- Collision checks cover every category except Colbert, The 36 and Magical.
- Roots cards never need a sharp childhood scene; use stories the family tells about you, things
  you kept, where the family comes from, grandparents, words and habits from where you grew up,
  what people expected of you.
- Screen every card for what it assumes about the player's life: group size (friend groups of four
  at most are common), partner, family, money, children.
- An "X or Y" contrast inside a card can be the point, not a two-questions fault.
- An owner's example shows tone and form, not the subject; spread the options over different
  subjects unless the owner says to stay on it.
- Before briefing, take the owner's examples apart (what answer they draw, how long, what feeling)
  and drop any rule that would rule out the owner's own example.
- Writing for a place starts with the place: who is there, when, what the moment is for.
- Anything made under Between Us carries its voice and vision; only the format adapts to the place.
- A blind comparison of writers shows equal counts per writer in every set.
