# Round 1 — models in words

A. The pause stays quiet; keeping the evening lives in the Menu.
At the end of a hand nothing new appears under the card: the same figure as during play. Keeping the evening becomes the Menu's first line, two taps from any moment the evening happens to end, and that line carries the once-only hint that teaches it.
Why it is good: the breather is truly calm, and there is one way to keep the evening, the same whether you stop mid-hand or at a pause.

B. The pause offers the one thing worth doing then.
At the end of a hand, Spotlight (which has nothing to show) gives its word to keeping the evening, in the same plain style, one tap. Mid-hand it is in the Menu too. Players learn it simply by seeing it at every breather.
Why it is good: the figure stays whole and balanced, the breather teaches the feature without a hint, and it takes one tap.

C. The evening keeps itself.
No save button at the pause or anywhere a player has to remember: the app keeps the evening as you play, so closing the browser loses nothing, and it is waiting in Played next time. Taking it out as a file stays in Played. The first time a kept evening is waiting, one line says so.
Why it is good: zero taps, nothing to learn, and it fits evenings that end at a random moment. Cost: it changes how saving works, not only the looks.

In all three, Change leaves the end of a hand (Keeper proposal, named to the owner): The Deck stays one tap away through "Selected deck" above the card.
