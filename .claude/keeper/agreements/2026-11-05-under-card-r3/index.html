What each option changes

L · Sorted by function

For the player: under the card is only what you use while playing, in the order you use it. The gold Next card; right under it, how you draw (One card or Three, a switch that stays as you set it, underlined when chosen); then Spotlight. One centred column. Back and Menu stop competing with Next.

The quietest of the three. Three as a switch is clear but small. Back only shows after a draw, so someone who wants to go two cards back needs to swipe.

M · One gold control

For the player: everything that plays the game is one object. Spotlight and Three are squares cut into the ends of the gold bar, so in the dark, or when you're reading for others, there is one thing to find. Three's end stays pressed in while it's on. The gold bar is balanced, with a function at each end.

Spotlight and Three are now gold, the same colour as Next, so a quick thumb can hit an end instead of the middle. The ends need their names under them, which adds a line.

N · Rethink: the card is the controller

For the player: you play by touching the card itself, the way you'd handle a real one. It grows to fill the tall screen and nothing sits under it.

  • Next card: tap the card (this already works today; it becomes the main way).
  • Spotlight: hold the card and it takes the stage.
  • Back: swipe right (already works today).
  • Three: set in The Deck, with how the evening draws.
  • Twist and star: on the card.
  • Count and coloured line: the card's bottom edge, with the count under it.
  • Menu: top right.
  • Hints: written into the foot of the card on the first visit.

No visible gold Draw: anyone who skips the first-visit hint has to guess. A phone passed from hand to hand gets touched by accident, and every touch on the card can now draw or spotlight. Three moves away from the draw, which the vision asks for.
