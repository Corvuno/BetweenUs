Played and Favourites, round 1

The inside of Played and Favourites, as they are now and in three new shapes, on the phone and on PC. Taken from the live app with its own fonts and colours: five cards drawn, two starred, two saved ("Friday with Sam", and "Work lunch", the one playing now).

The cards differ from picture to picture because the app draws them at random; the evening is the same. Same in every option: the boxed buttons go, and Export, Import and Clear become quiet words in a footer like the menu's. "Export log" and "Import log" lose the word "log". Clear reads "Clear the list", since it is the one that can't be undone. "LIVE" becomes "playing now". The pencil and cross become the words Rename and Delete. A saved one shows when it was saved, because the app records that. On PC both open in the same left-hand panel as the menu, instead of a small box in the middle. Tap a picture to open it full size.

What each option does

Now

Three boxed buttons come before the cards. Saved sits under the list, so every card you draw pushes it further down. "LIVE", the pencil and the cross don't match the rest of the app. On PC it's a small box with the text pressed against the colour bars.

A · Cards first

The cards come first, each in a frame like the menu's rows. The tools sit quietly under them, then Saved. It's the plainest step from today: everything stays where you'd look for it.

Saved still moves down as the list grows, but the tools no longer push it further.

B · Three sides

One screen with three sides you pick at the top: Drawn, Saved, Starred, each with its count. Saved never disappears under a long list, and everything you keep is in one place.

Favourites stops being its own screen, so the menu loses its Favourites line, a change to the menu you just approved. A side you're not on is one tap away.

C · A spread of cards (the rethink)

What you drew lies like small cards on the table, two across on the phone and three on PC, each topped in its own colour. It's the most "this game" of the three and the easiest to scan.

Long questions take more lines in a narrow card. With an odd number of cards one cell stays empty. It's the most new work.
