# Under the card: critique before round 6 (#113)

Owner, 2026-11-05: "It looks out of place. It does not match the rest of the aesthetic. Rearranging
what is there is not going to change that. It's the fundamentals… something not Art Deco enough.
Everything feels like it belongs, this does not. It's an afterthought." "Controls are situational,
not universal." Back: "Remove it, might not miss it at all." Three and Twist: "somewhere not that
space" (a drawer, the menu, The Deck, a new drawer of card modifiers…). Spotlight is a view of the
card, never a place to hide things.

Picture: now-start-and-end.png (start card, last card of a hand).

## What makes the top Art Deco (the parts that belong)
- Built from line: the logo is parallel hairlines of stepped lengths around one centre; the
  ornament is the structure.
- Nested frames: the start card is a frame inside a frame over a lattice; the card has corner lines.
- One vertical axis: logo, tagline, Selected deck, card all stack on the centre line.
- Lines end on purpose: hard ends and stepped lengths.
- One display letter (Poiret One) for the name.

## Why the lower part doesn't belong
1. It is built from stock interface parts, coloured in the deck's colours: a filled call-to-action
   button with a diagonal gradient, two outlined square icon buttons with stock glyphs (arrow,
   three lines), a segmented progress bar with rounded ends, and an icon-and-label toolbar row.
   The top is ornament that happens to work; the bottom is a toolbar that happens to be gold.
2. Fill against line: the gold button is the only large filled shape on the screen and the
   heaviest thing on it, heavier than the card. Everything else is drawn in line.
3. Horizontal bands instead of an axis: four strips (bars, count, button row, play row), each with
   its own width, edges and logic. The top is one centred composition; the bottom is a stack of
   toolbars.
4. Soft lines: the rules around the play row fade out at both ends, the "elegant default"; the
   logo's lines are stepped and end hard.
5. Four type voices in a short space: thin numerals, plain capitals (button), wide capitals
   (row), italic hints.
6. Not situational: on the start card four of the five controls are shown dimmed and dead.

## What works and stays
- The count in the logo's letter between hairlines: the one piece that already speaks the
  language.
- One clear main action (Draw), and symmetry as the basis (agreement 2026-10-13).
- Hints exist because players didn't know what the controls do (2026-10-13); they stay in some form.
- Celadon as the one accent for controls was the owner's choice (2026-10-14).

## Proposed brief for round 6 (for the owner to confirm, not yet agreed)
- Draw the controls in the logo's own vocabulary (line, steps, nested frames, one axis), not as
  interface parts.
- Show only what the moment needs (start card, a card, Three's options, end of hand).
- Fewer type voices.
- Every element's place and every word argued.
