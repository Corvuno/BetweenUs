Under the card, round 6

Four designs, each on its own idea, built from the critique you agreed with: the area under the card is made of stock app parts coloured gold, while the rest of the app is made of Art Deco line work.

The brief

  • Draw the controls in the app's own vocabulary: the logo's stepped hairlines, the start card's frame inside a frame, one centre line.
  • Show only what the moment needs, with no empty spots and the symmetry kept.
  • Fewer type styles: the logo's letter for the count and the action, the small capitals for labels, italic only for first-visit hints.
  • A reason for every place and every word.

Now

A · The mirror

The screen gets two bookends: the logo's ornament at the top, and the same ornament at the bottom, so the card sits between two matching pieces.

  • Ornament: the logo's own lines (long lines fading outward, stepped half-strength pairs, short outer lines), so it belongs at a glance.
  • Count: in the ornament's centre, where the logo has its diamond, because the count is the centre of a hand.
  • Action: in the logo's letter, under the centre, the largest word on the lower half, as the main thing to tap.
  • Left and right slots: at the ornament's two ends, where its lines come from.

The action is lettering, not a filled button. That's the most Art Deco choice, but the least obvious "tap here" for a first-timer. Its tap area has to be larger than it looks.

B · The frame

The start card's frame inside a frame becomes the action: a framed plate under the card, in the same two line strengths.

  • Plate: the start card's double frame (outer line strong, inner line faint), so the action is drawn like the deck's own card back.
  • Count: set into the plate's top edge, like a number on a plaque.
  • Action: in the logo's letter, inside the plate.
  • Left and right slots: outside the plate at its middle height, just the symbol and its word, with no frame of their own (no box around a single control).

The plate is the only framed object under the card, so it reads clearly as the button. But it's a box, and the start card above is also a framed box, so at the start of a hand the two frames echo each other strongly.

C · The steps

Deco's stepped skyline as the structure: three tiers that rise to the count.

  • Top tier: the count, the highest point, like the top of a building.
  • Middle tier: the two side slots, standing on its two halves.
  • Bottom tier: the gold action, the one filled shape, with stepped shoulders where the middle tier meets it.

The heaviest of the four: the gold base keeps the weight the critique called too much, though its shape now belongs. The outlines of the tiers add more line than A or B.

D · Rethink: turn it to show it

Used differently: Spotlight stops being a button. Turning the phone on its side puts the question on stage, and turning it back brings you home. Menu moves into "Selected deck" at the top, the door that already opens The Deck.

  • Spotlight: the turn of the phone (new). Spotlight already lies sideways for the table; now the turn itself opens it.
  • Menu: the lines mark beside "Selected deck".
  • Under the card: only the count, one gold hairline as wide as the card, and the action written under it, like a signature line.
  • End of a hand: Change and Save at the line's two ends.

Turning doesn't work on a phone lying flat on the table, or with screen rotation locked, and you use Spotlight on the table. It would need a second way in, which brings back a control. People also have to learn the turn; the first-visit hint carries it once.
