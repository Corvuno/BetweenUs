After the first visit

First visit, with hints

What each option changes for the player

S · The gold bar

Everything you play with is one gold object: Spotlight, Next card, Three. One thing to find in the dark or when reading for others. Menu in the count, because Played, Favourites and Start over are about your hands.

Spotlight and Three are gold like Next, so a quick thumb can hit an end. Their names need a line under the bar.

T · In the order you use it

One centred column read top to bottom: draw, how you draw (One card or Three, a switch that stays as you set it), show. Menu with the deck at the top, which already opens The Deck. The calmest of the five.

Quiet; the switch is small. Spotlight sits furthest from the thumb's path.

U · On the card, under the card

Splits the controls by what they act on. What acts on this card (star, Twist, Spotlight) is on the card. Under it is only drawing: Next card with Back and Three either side, in balance. The rule is easy to learn: on the card means this card.

Spotlight becomes small text on the card's foot, for something used often. Back stays a permanent control, so this option has no undo.

V · Set in type

No filled button anywhere: the controls are lettering, like a title card. NEXT CARD in the logo's letter between two gold rules, Spotlight and Three on the same line either side. The most deco, and calm in a shared screenshot.

Lettering reads less obviously as "tap here" than a filled button, especially for a first-timer. The tap area has to be larger than the letters look.

W · Rethink: the ribbon

A deck box has a ribbon to lift the cards out. Here a gold ribbon hangs from the middle of the card with the count on it. You pull it down, or tap it, to draw, and it springs back. No button under the card. Against the overview:

  • Next card: pull or tap the ribbon.
  • Count: printed on the ribbon.
  • Spotlight and Three: either side of the ribbon, on the card's edge.
  • Twist, line, Back and Menu: as in the others.

A new gesture to learn; the first-visit hint carries it. The ribbon is narrower than a button, so in the dark it's a smaller target, though tapping works as well as pulling.
