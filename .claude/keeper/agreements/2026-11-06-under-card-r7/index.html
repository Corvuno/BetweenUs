1 · The hand

The rest of this hand lies face down under the question and gets thinner as you play. Tap it to draw. The coloured line runs along its edge as the progress (you said the line may stay, "could be nice with the card thing"). Back: swipe the question back into the hand it came from. Menu: the lines mark at "Selected deck", the label of the box the deck came in. Spotlight is the one plain control.

2 · The dial

A radio dial with one notch per question. Turn it on to the next card, back to go back; the numbers are the hand. Pressing the knob is Spotlight, "turning it up for the table". Its two feet: Menu on the left (it changes what's playing), the count on the right. Hints are printed on the dial's face, the way a radio names its stations.

3 · Rethink: the programme

The evening as a printed programme: the hand is a row of acts, I to V, with only numbers, no future questions. Tap the next act to go on, any earlier act to go back. The programme's title is the deck's name and opens Menu, as a programme's cover opens it. Spotlight is "house lights down" at its foot.

  • Next card → tap the next act
  • Back → tap an earlier act
  • Count → the row of acts
  • Spotlight → house lights at the foot
  • Menu → the programme's title
  • Hints → the programme's subtitle line

What each one costs

1 · The hand

It shows cards other than the current one, as backs, which your vision normally rules out (you allowed one or two such options). With hands of 20 or All, the stack can show only a few edges, so the line carries the progress.

2 · The dial

Turning is a new gesture, so a tap on a number must work too. It takes the most height, and its count shows twice (the notch and "3 / 5").

3 · The programme

With 20 acts the row needs to scroll or shrink. The deck's name moves from above the card into the programme, which leaves a gap at the top that still has to be designed.
