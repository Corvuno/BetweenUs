# Round 7 ideas, in words (before the gate)

## Under the card: three ideas
Each line says what the player does. Under the card in every idea: drawing, the hand's progress and
Spotlight (decided: Spotlight stays; Three and Twist leave this space; Three's setting is in The Deck).

1. **One action that speaks.** The player presses one control that says in words what will happen and
   where the hand is ("Next card, 3 of 5"; "End of hand"; "Tap to flip card"), with Spotlight as its
   only partner; Menu sits opposite Spotlight for balance; Back is a swipe; hints sit under the two
   partners on the first visit.
2. **The hand in front of you.** The player takes the next card from the hand itself: the cards left
   in this hand lie under the question as card backs and get fewer as you play, so the progress is what
   you see, not numbers; played cards slide onto a small pile, and tapping the pile takes the last one
   back (Back); Spotlight is the one plain control; Menu opens from "Selected deck" at the top (the
   box the deck came in); the empty start card shows the ways to play (owner liked it).
3. **Rethink: you choose which card to turn.** The whole hand lies face down under the question and the
   player decides which card to turn next, any of them, so drawing becomes choosing; turned cards stay
   face up and small, and tapping one brings it back (Back); the count is how many are still face down;
   Spotlight is the one plain control; Menu opens from the count.
   Against the overview: Next card → choose a face-down card; progress → face-down cards left; Back →
   tap a turned card; Spotlight → unchanged; Menu → from the count; Three → in The Deck (still works:
   turning a card offers three); hints → one line under the hand.

## Twist, a modifier like a die: two places
a. **Throw the die.** The player shakes the phone to throw a twist onto the question in front of
   them; a shake again throws a new one; a first-visit line tells it once; Spotlight shows the same.
b. **A die above the card.** A small die sits just above the card, outside it, next to where the
   question begins; tapping it throws a twist onto that question.

## Design-gate verdict (2026-11-06): BLOCK
- Ideas 2 and 3 are one idea (the hand as card backs under the question); only the pick order differs.
  Twist a and b are one idea (throw a twist); only the trigger differs.
- Idea 1 is the same parts with words on the button.
- Ideas 2 and 3 break the vision: "no card other than the current one is on screen, past or future";
  a pile of played cards and a row of backs both do. Choosing any card cuts across Arc (warm up, go
  deeper, come back).
- Back, Menu's handle and hint places were written as fixed; they are open and need a reason each.
- Twist above the card sits against the card ("no interaction elements on the card"); shaking can't
  be done while holding the phone up for others.
Not shown to the owner as options. Raised with the owner instead: the vision rule on past and future
cards decides whether "handling the hand" can exist at all.
