# Try two, round 1: models in words (before the gate)

What stays under the card in every idea: drawing the next card, Spotlight, the Menu, and the
hand's progress. Twist is out of this area (open, see decided-file). What differs is how the
player finds and uses them.

A. **One action, the rest whispers.** The player sees one thing to do, Next card, and reads how far
   the hand is from that same button (it fills as the hand goes); Spotlight and Menu are two quiet
   words they only notice when they look for them. Progress shows how far, not which colours.

B. **Sorted by what it changes.** The player finds everything that changes the evening in one place,
   with the deck they chose (the Menu joins "Selected deck"), and under the card only what acts on
   this card: Next card and Spotlight, with the count. Progress is the number only.

C. **Rethink: the table is the button.** There is no button: the whole empty space under the card is
   the surface the player taps to draw the next card, with the hand's progress written quietly in it;
   Spotlight and Menu are the only two other things there.

## Gate verdict on the first three: BLOCK (2026-11-06)
A and B were the same four parts at different volume and place; C was called a rethink without
saying part by part what changes, and a button with no edge is not "shown". Twist-out and
colour-out were Keeper choices carried through every option. Not shown to the owner.

# Round 1, second try: models in words

Fixed by the owner only: Three goes to The Deck, Back goes, the card is for reading only.

D. **One door.** Under the card the player finds only drawing and one door: Next card and Menu.
   Behind the door: Spotlight (as the first thing in it), and the hand's progress, which becomes
   Played, the list that already shows the colours. Twist goes completely. Hints: none, because
   both controls say what they are in their own word.
   Why: the area does one job (play) and everything you use now and then is behind one door.

E. **The hand keeps time.** Next card, Spotlight and Menu stay in view; progress stops being a
   constant gauge and speaks only when it matters, at halfway and for the last few cards, then goes
   quiet. Twist becomes a setting in The Deck next to Three: when it is on, some cards arrive with
   their twist already on them. Hints: Spotlight keeps its existing hint, "the question takes the
   stage".
   Why: progress is for pacing, so it shows at the moments pacing matters; Twist is a modifier,
   like Three, so it is set for the evening instead of pressed per card.

F. **Rethink: the phone's position does the work.** Holding the phone upright is reading: under the
   card only Next card, Menu and the count. Turning the phone sideways is showing: it goes straight
   into Spotlight, and Twist lives only there, where it already is today. On a PC, where nothing
   turns, Spotlight keeps a control.
   Part by part: Next card stays; Menu stays; ticks go, the count stays; Spotlight's control goes on
   phones (turning replaces it); Twist leaves this area and stays inside Spotlight; Back and Three
   are already gone. Hint: one, on the first visit, telling the player to turn the phone to show
   the question.
   Why: showing the card to others is a physical act, so the physical act opens it.

## Gate verdict on D, E, F: BLOCK (2026-11-06)
D put Spotlight behind the Menu, against the vision line "Three and Full screen belong right next to
the draw", and called Played a replacement for progress (it can't show how far). E kept the controls as
they are and invented a Twist feature. F made Spotlight an invisible gesture (rotation lock, car holder,
reading sideways). All three kept Next card and Menu with no owner words. Not shown to the owner.
Two blocked sets: per the two-miss rule, back to the owner with questions of fact and taste before a third.
