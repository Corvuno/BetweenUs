Mark options, third roundSeven curved, symmetric marks in the fleur-de-lis family. Each is shown in place under the real header (left) and at print scale (right).
1Fleur
Between Us
Everything on the table

The classical shape, redrawn thin: one upright petal, two petals swinging out and up, a band across the base and a three-part tail. Closest to the fleur-de-lis itself.

Most recognisable, and the one most tied to heraldry, the Scouts and New Orleans.

2Deco fleur
Between Us
Everything on the table

The same idea built from straight-edged kites, five of them fanned out from one point, over a band and a pointed tail. Fleur-like in outline, Art Deco in construction.

The most geometric of the set; the least curved.

3Lotus
Between Us
Everything on the table

Five petals fanned from a base, tallest in the middle, standing on two lines. Fleur-de-lis cousin from the other end of the world; the lotus is a core Deco motif.

Softer and fuller than the classic fleur, so it may read as a flower more than a crest.

4Curled fleur
Between Us
Everything on the table

Upright centre petal with the side petals ending in outward spirals, the way a lot of ornamental ironwork draws it.

The most ornamental and the most hand-drawn feeling; the spirals are the first thing to disappear at small size.

5Lyre
Between Us
Everything on the table

Two scrolled arms sweeping up and out from a centre stem, each ending in a small curl, with one filled diamond on top. Not a fleur, but the same family of curve and symmetry.

Reads as a lyre, though it can also read as a tulip bulb. The small curls at the top are the first thing to disappear at header size.

6Rosette
Between Us
Everything on the table

Eight pointed petals around a ring and dot, symmetric all the way round, not just left to right. No top, no bottom.

The only one that works at any rotation. Busier at the real header size than the single-axis marks.

7Rising arches
Between Us
Everything on the table

Three nested pointed arches with curved shoulders, each lighter than the last, standing on a line. The curved cousin of the straight arches in round two.

Gentlest and most architectural. The nested lines are faint, and the two inner ones may disappear at small size.
