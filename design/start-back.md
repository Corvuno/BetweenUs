# Start card back (#195)

The start card shows one of seven backs from `backs.js` (`.cb-streams`): turn by turn, bookmatched, cut areas in purple with glints, and round 13's threshold, radiant, four lamps (with glints) and winged door, never the same twice in a row. The owner keeps these for now; a proper card back, also for print, comes later. They sit behind the logo and the line
"Each question is a door, left slightly open." This record covers new backs for the start card only;
the end card (Intermezzo) may later get a design of its own.

## Where the topic stands

Parked. Rounds 13, 14 and 15 held nothing the owner enjoys. It comes back only with a new idea or new
references. The owner's verdict on the last round, in their words: "its neither a good card back, not
a good Art Deco graphic, nor a nice wallpaper for a image.. So no.." Earlier: round 13 was "pretty
pictures, not brilliant card backs"; round 14 "Roulette table much?".

## What a good card back is

Read from the card backs in the owner's reference folder (Anubis, Osiris, the owl deck, Rarebit, the
ornate backs, and the portrait frames the owner filed under Cards):

1. Full: the drawing covers the card edge to edge, with little empty dark.
2. The centre is the richest place: the emblem (an eye, a key, a medallion) is the most detailed spot.
3. The middle band is busy; the ends are calmer.
4. It reads at three distances: one shape from across the table, structure at arm's length, fine
   hatching up close.
5. Gold on one ground, with colour only in small touches.
6. Mirrored and framed. For the owner, a frame around a window is part of what makes a card.

## What the owner settled

- Symmetry as today's backs have it. The logo and the line stay upright; that they don't read
  upside down is fine.
- The logo and the line are built into the design, the way real cards build in their emblem: placed
  where the drawing gives them a logical reason to be ("not so much that its done, just make it so its
  logical in the design").
- The backs are one family, each with a clearly different design and look, fitting the app's theme.
  Colour may be added where wanted.
- Glow and glints are the Keeper's call: kept only where they help on a screen.

## What went wrong, and why

- A frame around a big empty window is a table or a mirror, not a card back. The empty middle is the
  opposite of property 2.
- Rings round an oval plus colour in alternating wedges or teeth read as a roulette wheel; red, green
  and blue in broad bands is gaming-table colour.
- Giving the logo a role (door jewel, lamp light, cartouche, the logo grown into a pattern) was not
  enough: the results were neither a good card back nor a good Art Deco graphic. Ticking properties
  does not make the whole good.

## Open
